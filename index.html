About me

Turning research into a clear business story.

I contributed to the StudyBuddy group venture through market research and the live presentation. My main responsibility was helping define the first customer segment and explaining why first-year and international university students in London represented a realistic and commercially relevant starting market.

The experience required me to analyse student needs, coordinate my section with the wider pitch and communicate evidence confidently to an audience. It strengthened both my business understanding and my ability to work effectively within a team.

Reflection

From contributing information to communicating value.

“Good teamwork is not simply dividing slides; it requires shared understanding and consistent decisions.”

When first starting the StudyBuddy project, it was easy to share ideas in small group discussions, but I didn't have much experience giving a business idea to a larger group. I also had to better link market research to sound commercial actions. My primary focus was consequently to be a better communicator, and to provide valuable evidence about the young people we were aiming to help.

My contribution was determining the target market and presenting target customer section. I explored first-year and international university students needs in London; loneliness, poor time management, lack of academic support and lack of forming academic relationships. This research facilitated the group to develop a specific customer profile rather than market the “all students”. It also supported StudyBuddy’s value proposition: an affordable platform that combines connection, accountability and structured peer support.

For the live pitch, I learned how to communicate what I researched and know, into a concise and understandable explanation. I rehearsed my points, incorporated the slide as a visual aide and emphasized the explanation of why the segment was commercially viable. I focused on the audience and the point of each of the remarks to prevent me from becoming nervous during the presentation. The photos and video show that I actively engaged with the market and communicated our market strategy in a professional manner.

This project also helped me develop my team work and problem solving skills. Making sure that my section flowed with the rest of the presentation to make it cohesive with the problem, value proposition, and business model. If the information was too general I contributed to making it a realistic early customer group. It meant to me that good teamwork isn't just about splitting slides, it's about making unanimous decisions and understanding.

Overall I feel better in my public speaking, more analytical in my market research and more aware of the importance of customer evidence in a venture. I would still need to work on using the numerical market information and being more spontaneous during questions, however. In future projects, I will start researching earlier, find more reliable sources, practice questions and get feedback from peers before presenting.
