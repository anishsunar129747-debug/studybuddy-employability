350-word reflection

From contributing information to communicating value.

“Good teamwork is not simply dividing slides; it requires shared understanding and consistent decisions.”

At the beginning of the StudyBuddy project, I was comfortable sharing ideas in small discussions, but I had limited experience presenting a business concept to a larger audience. I also needed to improve the way I connected market research with clear commercial decisions. My main goal was therefore to become a more confident communicator while contributing useful evidence about the students our venture intended to support.

My contribution focused on researching the target market and presenting the target-customer section. I examined the needs of first-year and international university students in London, including isolation, difficulty managing deadlines, limited academic support and the challenge of forming reliable study relationships. This research helped the group define a specific customer profile instead of describing “all students” as the market. It also supported StudyBuddy’s value proposition: an affordable platform that combines connection, accountability and structured peer support.

Preparing for the live pitch developed my communication skills because I had to turn detailed research into a short, understandable explanation. I practised the sequence of my points, used the slide as visual support and focused on explaining why the selected segment was commercially attractive. During the presentation, I managed nervousness by concentrating on the audience and the purpose of each point. The photographs and video provide evidence that I participated actively and communicated our market logic in a professional setting.

The project also strengthened my teamwork and problem-solving. I had to coordinate my section with the wider presentation so that it connected naturally with the problem, value proposition and business model. When information was too broad, I helped narrow it to a realistic early customer group. This showed me that good teamwork is not simply dividing slides; it requires shared understanding and consistent decisions.

Overall, I became more confident in public speaking, more analytical in market research and more aware of how customer evidence supports a venture. However, I still need to improve my use of numerical market data and respond more spontaneously during questions. In future projects, I will begin research earlier, use stronger sources, rehearse possible questions and seek peer feedback before the final presentation.
